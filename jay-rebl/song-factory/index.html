Strategy — Jay RebL

The Song Factory

A TikTok-first plan for turning one song into fifty pieces of content — built to find the fifteen seconds the internet refuses to scroll past, then attack it from every angle.

For discussion, not yet in motion
Back to Jay RebL

01 — The Core Shift

Build It Around the Song

Jay is the creator. The song is the content engine. Every video is one more reason for someone to encounter the same piece of music — not a new ask for attention every time.

"How do we get Jay RebL's music more views?"

"How do we make people stop scrolling because they need to know what happens next — and let the song be the payoff?"

TikTok is the primary machine here, not just another place to post. TikTok for Artists gives song-level data — views, the number of posts using a song, creator engagement — and TikTok's own 2025 Music Impact Report found that 84% of the songs that entered the Billboard Global 200 in 2024 had gone viral on TikTok first. Instagram Reels is the second layer: same clips, more polish, built for brand rather than discovery.

The Song Content Factory

03 — Why TikTok Specifically

The Sound-Reuse Flywheel

On most platforms, a video's reach ends with its own viewer. TikTok's sound page breaks that: a viewer can tap the sound, see everyone else using it, and post their own video with it — carrying the song to an audience Jay never posted to directly.

04 — Who Does What

Platform Roles

Not treated identically. Each platform gets one job.

TikTok

Discovery + viral testing

The laboratory — post aggressively, find the pattern, let the sound page do the distribution.

IG Reels

Brand-building

Take the TikTok concepts that already proved out and post a more polished version.

FB Reels

Mass distribution

Especially for emotional, nostalgic, or culturally-rooted clips.

YouTube Shorts

Discovery + search

A durable, searchable home for winning clips.

X

Conversation, not distribution

Opinions, lyric lines, commentary — personality, not the pitch.

SoundCloud

Listening destination

Where the full catalog lives — not where the audience gets built.

05 — The Deconstruction

Ten Angles, One Song

Every song gets attacked from all ten before we decide what's working. Same song, ten different reasons to watch.

Worked example: "Nuff We Face" — real lyrics, one line pulled per angle below — so this reads as ten actual shot ideas instead of ten abstract categories.
01

Performance

Jay performs the hook, straight — no framing, just the moment.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“A nuff we face, a nuff we face, every day — it's like a different case, a different case, every day.”

Sing the hook once, full-frame, no talking before or after. Cut on the last word.

02

Story

“I wrote this after …” — the song plays as the ending, not the pitch.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Daddy hardly have education, so job quickly get delete … them not know what we even go a bed, but them not know we even eat.”

Tell the story behind that verse first, in Jay's own words — then let the lyric land as the punchline, straight into the hook.

03

Question

“What does this line mean to you?” — the comment section becomes the content.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Rather walk away than take them and lose the way.”

Post the line on its own with the question in the caption. The debate in the comments is the second video.

04

Street Experiment

Jay asks a stranger a question, then plays the line it inspired.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“The more you start to overcome, the more you start seeing.”

Ask a stranger “what's something you overcame that people don't see?” — then play this line right after their answer.

05

Emotional Reaction

Someone hearing the lyric for the first time, unscripted.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Oh Father, please make a way. Help us overcome these trials we face, day by day.”

Play just the prayer bridge for someone, camera on their face, no warning what's coming.

06

Challenge

“Finish this line …” — the audience writes the next verse.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Big up all the youths who overcome the struggles in the streets. SIP to all the one them where the …”

Cut right before “the system did defeat” and ask people to guess or finish it in the comments.

07

Behind the Song

Jay explains the line people keep misreading.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“SIP to all the one them where the system did defeat.”

Explain “SIP” is R.I.P. — this is a line for people the system took, not a lyric about a place.

08

Fan Version

Someone else performs it, credited back to the original.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“A nuff we face, a nuff we face, every day …”

Hand the hook to another vocalist over the same riddim — duet it against Jay's original for the side-by-side.

09

Remix

Another artist adds a verse, a genre flip, a new arrangement.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Big up all the youths who overcome the struggles in the streets …”

Invite another artist to answer that line with a verse of their own struggle, same riddim, new voice.

10

Controversy

“People keep telling me this lyric is wrong …” — and why it isn't.

Applied to "Nuff We Face"

“Yow me born come see nothing, dawg, me barely change me ways.”

Address people who hear this as giving up — and explain it's naming the starting line, not accepting it.

07 — Applied to "Cover Me"

The First 30 Days

One song, four weeks, roughly fifty posts. We're not committing to this being the song forever — we're committing to running the full process on one song, once, so the next cycle runs faster.

Week 1

Discovery

Post 1–2x/day, testing the field

Run through as many of the ten angles as we can shoot: performance, story, question, behind-the-song, and controversy first — they need the least production.

  • No follower targets. We're only looking for content-market fit.
  • Every post gets logged: angle, hook, hold rate, shares.

Week 2

Widen the Field

Add the harder-to-shoot angles

Street experiment, challenge, fan version, remix. By day 14 we should have a real read on which 2–3 angles are pulling.

  • Cut nothing yet — we don't have enough data until this point.

Week 3

Double Down

Kill the bottom performers, scale the top

Take the top 2–3 angles by shares-per-1,000-views and shoot 3–5 variations of each. If one clip is clearly outperforming, that becomes the week's whole focus.

Week 4

Scale & Debrief

Push the winning format, write the playbook

Full production behind whatever's working. If a participatory format (finish-the-line, fan remix) is gaining, this is when we manufacture the bigger version of it — a "we need 1,000 people to help finish this" moment.

  • Close the cycle with a short written debrief: what worked, what we'd never repeat, and which song runs next.

09 — For This Session

Decisions to Leave With

Confirm the pilot song

“Cover Me,” or a different pick — Jay knows the catalog and the story behind each track better than any play count does.

Claim TikTok for Artists

Verify Jay's catalog on TikTok's own artist platform — it's the source for sound-level data (views, posts using the song, creator engagement) this whole plan depends on.

Set the posting cadence

2–4 short videos a day for 30 days is the target above — confirm what's realistic given who's shooting and editing.

Assign the roles

Who directs concepts, who shoots/edits, who handles comments and outreach — even if it's Jay wearing two of those hats to start.

Agree the north-star metric

Shares per 1,000 views, tracked per post from day one — so week 3's cuts are based on data, not gut feeling.

Pick the start date

Day 1 of the 30-day cycle — everything above is dated from whenever we actually start filming.
